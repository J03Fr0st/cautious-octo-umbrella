# Our family's history, captured in one place relatives can explore

> Drafted by an agent from an interview with Joe; the human's answers are authoritative.

## Problem

What we know about our family is scattered across people's memories, papers, chats and files. Much of it is held by older relatives, and it will be lost if it isn't recorded. Nowhere shows the whole family, so it is hard to see or explain how people are related.

## Outcome

Our family has one private, visual family tree that relatives can easily browse, and it holds everyone we know about. Relatives can add to it and correct it themselves, so the knowledge is saved before it is lost.

## Who

- **Joe**: the owner, who builds and looks after the tree.
- **Invited family members**: they view the tree and edit it directly. Only invited relatives can see it.
- **Older relatives**: the main source of the knowledge to capture, directly or through other family members.
- **Other genealogy tools**: sources of existing data through GEDCOM import.

## Why now

Older relatives hold family knowledge that isn't written down anywhere. The goal is to capture it while they can still share it.

## Non-goals

- **DNA results or matching with other trees**: out of scope.
- **Searching historical records** (census, birth, archive records): out of scope.
- **Photos, document scans and stories for each person**: out of scope for now.
- **A product for other families**: it serves only our family, with no outside accounts and no billing.

## Success signal

- **Family actually uses it**: invited relatives visit and explore the tree on their own, without being walked through it.
- **Whole tree captured**: everyone in the family that we know about is recorded, with their relationships.
- **Works on phones**: relatives can browse and navigate the tree comfortably on a phone, not only on a desktop.

## Assumptions

- Relatives are added by invitation, and anyone who isn't invited can't see anything. Owner: Joe.
- People and relationships get into the tree three ways: typed in on the site, imported from GEDCOM files, and added or corrected by relatives. Owner: Joe.
- The tree will hold roughly 100 to 1,000 people. Owner: Joe.
- Joe is the only one who needs to administer it (inviting people and managing access). Owner: Joe.

## Open questions

- How will "family actually uses it" be measured? For example, how many relatives visit or edit within the first few months? Owner: Joe.
- Direct editing means a mistaken or accidental change can overwrite correct information. Is it enough to see who changed what and undo it? Owner: Joe.
- What should be recorded for each person beyond name and relationships? For example dates, places, notes. Owner: Joe.
- Are there privacy rules for living relatives, such as hiding birth dates from some viewers? Owner: Joe.
